# Bet on Microsoft agents, hedge with code

For a JavaScript/full-stack engineer choosing between the two ecosystems for 2026–2031, **Microsoft's pro-code Power Platform and agent stack is the better primary bet**, and Salesforce is a narrower, higher-ceiling secondary option. On a like-for-like Dice snapshot taken 2026-11-09, US postings are close: "Power Platform" shows **925** and "Salesforce developer" **800**, and Copilot Studio (**382**) appears in more postings than Agentforce (**249**). Salesforce's own ecosystem shrank **13% in 2026**, with the losses concentrated in the developer and admin roles a newcomer would compete for. Microsoft's installed base, by contrast, gives it a long adoption runway: over 450M M365 seats, about 7% of them carrying Copilot. In the DMV the picture splits. Power Platform has far more jobs physically located in the region, roughly 280 against about 130 for Salesforce admin, but that demand comes from federal contractors and is heavily clearance-skewed. Salesforce's DMV results are mostly remote-eligible and look less clearance-bound. Because your job-search tool drops clearance jobs, plan to work the non-cleared civilian and Public Trust slice of the DMV Power Platform market, and treat remote Salesforce roles as a parallel pool. Hard data is thin on both sides. There is no US-only time series for either platform, and Microsoft publishes nothing like Salesforce's 10K ecosystem report, so the comparison below rests on one-day posting snapshots, vendor earnings, and recruiter data.

## Both ecosystems are polarizing: AI and architecture grow while generic build work shrinks

Salesforce has the better-documented labor market, and that documentation shows a reshuffle, not growth. Global Salesforce job demand fell **37% in 2024**, recovered **8% in 2025**, and fell **13% again in 2026**. Sales Cloud dropped 13%, Service Cloud 21%, Marketing Cloud 28%, and Tableau 32% ([Salesforce Ben, Oct 16 2026](https://www.salesforceben.com/salesforce-job-demand-is-down-13-should-you-still-bother-in-2027/)). Inside that shrinking pie, **Agentforce demand rose 63% globally and 87% in North America**, and Agentforce's share of listings went from 5% to 9% ([Salesforce Ben, Oct 19 2026](https://www.salesforceben.com/agentforce-jobs-are-booming-while-projects-stall-in-pilot/)). The supply side is the bigger problem for a newcomer. In 2025, **developer demand fell 12% while developer supply rose 20%**. Technical architect demand rose 27% against supply growth of only 2–4%. Overall talent supply runs about **3.4x demand** ([Salesforce Ben, Oct 2025](https://www.salesforceben.com/the-state-of-the-salesforce-job-market-in-2025-26-according-to-the-10k-report/)). Salesforce Ben's 2030 outlook expects basic Apex/LWC developers and generic consultants to be squeezed and calls traditional entry paths "the weakest" ([Salesforce Ben, Jul 2026](https://www.salesforceben.com/what-will-salesforce-careers-look-like-in-2030/)).

Salesforce the company is a leading indicator here. Engineering headcount has been **"mostly flat" at about 15,000 for two years**, which Benioff credits to coding agents ([ITPro](https://www.itpro.com/business/business-strategy/marc-benioff-salesforce-software-engineering-hiring-flat-ai)). Support staff fell from about 9,000 to about 5,000 because of AI agents ([ABC7](https://abc7news.com/post/4000-salesforce-customer-service-jobs-cut-due-artificial-intelligence-ceo-marc-benioff-says/17722910/)). Revenue still grows **11%**, but the mix matters. The core "Agentforce Apps" clouds grew only **8%** in constant currency, while Data 360, MuleSoft, Informatica, and Tableau grew **20%** ([Salesforce 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001108524/000110852426000187/crm-q2fy27xexhibit991.htm)). Ecosystem jobs are following the money toward data, integration, and agents and away from classic CRM configuration.

Microsoft's side has no 10K equivalent, so its trend has to be read from the product. The company stopped reporting Power Platform monthly active users after mid-2024, when it had **48M MAU, up 40%** ([msdynamicsworld](https://msdynamicsworld.com/node/31222)). It now talks about agents and data. In FY26 Q3, **nearly 90% of the Fortune 500 had active low-code agents**, Copilot Credit consumption nearly doubled quarter over quarter, and Fabric reached **35,000 paid customers (+60%)** ([Microsoft FY26 Q3](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/investor/events/fy-2026/earnings-fy-2026-q3)). By FY26 Q4 there were more than **30M paid M365 Copilot seats** ([Microsoft FY26 Q4](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/investor/events/fy-2026/earnings-fy-2026-q4)). One warning sign stands out: Dynamics 365 growth slowed from 22% to **13%** across FY26 Q3–Q4 amid renewal friction ([Futurum](https://futurumgroup.com/insights/microsoft-q4-fy-2026-ai-demand-accelerates-azure-and-enterprise-growth/)). That points to softer demand for classic D365-plus-Power-Platform consulting.

Job titles are already changing. US postings now read "Power Platform Developer with Copilot Studio," "Copilot Studio Lead Developer," and "Senior Power Platform / Copilot Studio Developer," and many require 2+ years of Copilot Studio experience ([Apex Systems](https://www.apexsystems.com/job/3052282_usa/microsoft-copilot-studio--power-platform-developer); [career.com](https://www.career.com/job/bcforward/senior-power-platform-copilot-studio-developer/j202605060833156232108)). The same polarization shows up on both sides. Gartner forecasts low-code at **$58.2B by 2029 (14.1% CAGR)**, driven by agentic AI ([Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/documents/7146430)). It also predicts **over 40% of agentic AI projects will be canceled by end-2027** ([Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-06-25-gartner-predicts-over-40-percent-of-agentic-ai-projects-will-be-canceled-by-end-of-2027)). The likely result is a 2027–2028 shakeout that punishes pilot-only work and rewards people who can get agents onto real data in production. Salesforce Ben reports that nearly half of Agentforce adopters are still in pilot ([Salesforce Ben](https://www.salesforceben.com/agentforce-jobs-are-booming-while-projects-stall-in-pilot/)).

Dice is a contract-heavy subset of the market, so read ratios, not totals. SimplyHired, for example, shows **2,753** "Salesforce developer" jobs where Dice shows 800 ([SimplyHired](https://www.simplyhired.com/search?q=Salesforce+developer&l=)).

Nationally, Power Platform slightly leads on developer-type phrasing (Salesforce developer : Power Platform = **0.86:1**). Salesforce pulls ahead about **2:1** only when admin roles are added, and admin is the role Salesforce Ben calls oversupplied and recruiters call "disappearing" ([Salesforce Ben](https://www.salesforceben.com/salesforce-job-postings-more-than-doubled-in-16-months-what-changed/)). On the AI-agent products, Copilot Studio beats Agentforce **382 to 249**. These keywords measure different things, though. "Power Platform" also matches SharePoint, Dynamics, and Power BI roles, and Copilot Studio often appears as a nice-to-have in M365 help-desk postings, so the Microsoft counts run high.

The DMV column needs a correction. Dice's radius search counted 23–35% of US postings as DMV, which is implausible for one metro and means remote-eligible jobs are leaking in. Sampling the first page of results resolves it. **28 of 30 Power Platform results were physically in DC/MD/VA**, posted by SAIC, CACI, Booz Allen, and Redhorse. By contrast, **17 of 30 Salesforce admin results and 20 of 30 Agentforce results were remote** ([Dice DMV Power Platform](https://www.dice.com/jobs?q=Power%20Platform&location=Washington,%20DC,%20USA&latitude=38.9072&longitude=-77.0369&countryCode=US&locationPrecision=City&radius=50&radiusUnit=mi); [Dice DMV Agentforce](https://www.dice.com/jobs?q=Agentforce&location=Washington,%20DC,%20USA&latitude=38.9072&longitude=-77.0369&countryCode=US&locationPrecision=City&radius=50&radiusUnit=mi)). LinkedIn returns **3,000+** broad-match "Power Platform" results for the Washington DC–Baltimore area. Guidehouse posted about 11 of the first 60, including five "Microsoft Copilot Studio & Power Platform Solution Engineer" roles, and Accenture Federal, Deloitte, Booz Allen, SAIC, and CACI followed ([LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/jobs/search?keywords=%22Power%20Platform%22&location=Washington%20DC-Baltimore%20Area)). No comparable Salesforce DMV count could be pulled because LinkedIn rate-limited the request.

## Clearance cuts the DMV Power Platform advantage roughly in half

The DMV Power Platform market leans on clearances, which matters directly for a tool that filters them out. ClearanceJobs, where every posting requires a clearance, lists **458** quoted "Power Platform" jobs nationally, about half the Dice US count. It lists **502** "Salesforce" jobs, about 27% of the combined Dice Salesforce developer and admin count ([ClearanceJobs Power Platform](https://www.clearancejobs.com/jobs?keywords=%22Power%20Platform%22); [ClearanceJobs Salesforce](https://www.clearancejobs.com/jobs?keywords=Salesforce)). The two boards use different query syntax, so these ratios are directional only, but the direction is clear. In the LinkedIn DMV sample, only **7 of 60** Power Platform titles named a clearance. Many other results were at sites such as the Pentagon, Quantico, and Fort Belvoir, where clearances are standard. A reasonable working assumption, which the research did not verify, is that **30–50% of DMV Power Platform contractor roles** need a Secret, TS, or DHS suitability clearance ([LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/jobs/search?keywords=%22Power%20Platform%22&location=Washington%20DC-Baltimore%20Area)).

Your filter in `src/lib/filters.js` is also wider than "active clearance required." It drops postings that say "clearance eligible" or "public trust clearance required." SAIC's Arlington Senior Power Platform Developer role ($120K–$160K, Secret minimum with ability to obtain TS/SCI) is the kind of posting it removes ([Dice](https://www.dice.com/jobs?q=Power%20Platform&countryCode=US)). That is correct if you cannot get a clearance. But if you are a US citizen willing to be sponsored, "eligible" roles are a door you are choosing to close, and clearance adds roughly **$10K–$40K at senior levels**. That figure is inferred from cleared DMV Power Platform postings at $135K–$175K against a non-cleared average of about $120K ([Dice](https://www.dice.com/job-detail/7f53091d-c725-49fa-abbd-59b60ef94667); [Salary.com](https://www.salary.com/research/salary/position/power-platform-developer-salary/dc)).

The non-clearance opportunity in the DMV is real but needs targeting. On the Microsoft side, civilian-agency and commercial-practice roles at **Guidehouse, Deloitte, Booz Allen, Accenture Federal, and Planet Technologies** (a Microsoft partner) appear in DC, McLean, and Arlington. Remote roles such as Koniag's "Developer – Power Platform (REMOTE)" also exist ([LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/jobs/search?keywords=%22Power%20Platform%22&location=Washington%20DC-Baltimore%20Area)). Demand rests on broad federal adoption. The September 2025 GSA OneGov deal gives agencies an estimated **$3.1B in first-year savings** and **12 months of free M365 Copilot for G5 customers**, priced for up to 36 months ([GSA](https://www.gsa.gov/about-gsa/newsroom/news-releases/multibillion-dollar-gsa-onegov-agreement-with-microsoft-brings-steep-discounts-09022025)). M365 Copilot reached GCC High in December 2025 ([Microsoft Tech Community](https://techcommunity.microsoft.com/blog/publicsectorblog/microsoft-365-copilot-is-now-available-in-gcc-high/4473310)). Copilot Studio in GCC High and DoD still lags, which keeps cleared DoD work on classic Power Apps for now and leaves civilian GCC agencies as the most open lane for agent-building jobs.

Salesforce's DMV demand is concentrated in a few large programs. The **Army awarded a $5.6B-ceiling, 10-year IDIQ** (January 2026) to Salesforce's national-security subsidiary for its 28,000-person recruiting enterprise ([Salesforce IR](https://investor.salesforce.com/news/news-details/2026/U-S--Army-Awards-Salesforce-5-6B-Contract-to-Accelerate-Military-Modernization-and-Department-of-War-Readiness)). **The VA signed a $1.6B-ceiling Agentic ELA** (July 2026) for triage, benefits verification, and scheduling ([Salesforce](https://www.salesforce.com/news/press-releases/2026/07/24/missionforce-transforms-veteran-care/)). The Army work leans cleared. The VA is a civilian agency, so more of its Salesforce work plausibly needs only Public Trust, though that is unverified. Both figures are ceilings, and the VA deal's option years end around 2029. The region itself is recovering from a hard 2025. The Washington MSA lost **62,100 federal jobs (−16.5%)** and about 103,900 jobs in total from January 2025 to January 2026 ([Patch/COG](https://patch.com/virginia/mclean/dc-region-lost-62-100-federal-jobs-year-cog-report-finds)), yet ranked **#2 nationally for tech postings** in May 2026 ([Datawrapper/CompTIA](https://datawrapper.dwcdn.net/AMYdo/1/)). The DOGE cuts hit advisory consulting hardest. Platform implementation work on enterprise SaaS proved comparatively resilient.

## Pay is at parity mid-career; Salesforce pays more at the top, Microsoft transfers better

At mid level, the two ecosystems pay about the same. Nationally, Salesforce developers earn a median of about **$120K–$150K** across survey and recruiter sources, and technical architects **$182.5K** (Mason Frank median) with contract rates of **$131–$208/hr** ([Salesforce Ben salaries 2026](https://www.salesforceben.com/the-state-of-salesforce-salaries-in-2026/)). Power Platform developers average **$129K** on ZipRecruiter ([ZipRecruiter](https://www.ziprecruiter.com/Salaries/Power-Platform-Developer-Salary)), and Power Platform solution architects about **$146K** ([ZipRecruiter](https://www.ziprecruiter.com/Jobs/Power-Platform-Solution-Architect)). Tenth Revolution's postings range up to **$150K–$200K** for a hybrid VA/DC Power Platform engineer ([Tenth Revolution](https://www.tenthrevolution.com/job/power-platform-engineer-2/)). In DC, a Salesforce developer averages **$129.7K** on Indeed ([Indeed](https://www.indeed.com/salaries/salesforce-developer-Salaries,-Washington-DC)) and a Power Platform developer **$120.4K** on Salary.com ([Salary.com](https://www.salary.com/research/salary/position/power-platform-developer-salary/dc)).

Microsoft's weak spot is contract pricing. US W2 rates for Copilot Studio work cluster around **$42–$75/hr**, overlapping Polish B2B rates of $43–$46/hr ([career.com](https://www.career.com/job/bayside-solutions/copliot-studio-engineer/j202604062003236560791); [justjoin.it](https://justjoin.it/job-offer/experis-manpower-group-copilot-studio-developer-wroclaw-net)). That points to real offshore pressure on routine agent builds. Salesforce's AI premium is better documented: one recruiter reports Agentforce developer roles paying **$15K–$35K more** than non-AI equivalents ([KORE1](https://www.kore1.com/salesforce-developer-salary-guide/)). Across all industries, postings that list AI skills advertise about **28% higher pay** ([Lightcast](https://lightcast.io/resources/blog/beyond-the-buzz-press-release-2025-07-23)).

For a JS engineer, how well skills carry over is the deciding factor. Microsoft's pro-code surface is ordinary JavaScript and TypeScript. Power Apps "code apps" are **React + TypeScript + Vite** apps that run inside the platform ([VP365](https://site-vp365-ai.pages.dev/blog/power-apps-code-apps-what-pro-developers-need-to-know/)). PCF components use React and Fluent ([Microsoft](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/power-platform/blog/power-apps/react-and-fluent-based-virtual-code-components-are-now-generally-available/)). The **M365 Agents SDK ships TypeScript packages with Express hosting** ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/javascript/api/overview/agents-overview?view=agents-sdk-js-latest)). All of that carries over to non-Microsoft AI engineering. Salesforce's JS entry points are also real. LWC gained full TypeScript support in Spring '26 ([Salesforce Ben](https://www.salesforceben.com/9-features-for-salesforce-developers-in-the-spring-26-release/)), and SFCC commerce roles ask for advanced JavaScript at about **$110K–$142K** ([ZipRecruiter SFCC](https://www.ziprecruiter.com/Jobs/SFCC)). Even so, a Salesforce developer must also learn Apex, SOQL, the metadata model, and Agent Script, and none of that transfers outside Salesforce. Salesforce's tooling also changes fast: its LWC MCP tools were superseded within months ([Salesforce LWC MCP docs](https://developer.salesforce.com/docs/platform/lwc/guide/mcp-intro.html)). Both platforms now speak MCP, so TypeScript MCP-server skills are portable to either ([Microsoft Learn Agent 365](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-agent-365/developer/); [Salesforce Summer '26](https://developer.salesforce.com/blogs/2026/06/the-salesforce-developers-guide-to-the-summer-26-release)).

Microsoft reset its certifications in 2026. **PL-400, PL-200, PL-600, AI-102, and AZ-204 all retired in 2026**, and the new expert credential is **AB-100, Agentic AI Business Solutions Architect**. AB-100 covers Copilot Studio, Foundry, MCP, and A2A, and it requires an associate certification first: AB-620, AB-410, or AI-103 ([Microsoft Learn retirements](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/credentials/support/retired-certification-exams); [Microsoft Learn AB-100](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/credentials/certifications/exams/ab-100/)). Salesforce is renaming most of its credentials around Agentforce and retiring 24 of them on Feb 1, 2027. Recruiters say certifications alone no longer land entry roles ([Salesforce Ben](https://www.salesforceben.com/what-will-salesforce-careers-look-like-in-2030/)).

## Conclusion

The conventional framing, "Salesforce pays more, Microsoft is everywhere," misses what matters for this reader. Salesforce's premium sits at the architect and forward-deployed-engineer level. Salesforce's FDE postings ask for 6+ years of experience ([The Muse](https://www.themuse.com/jobs/salesforce/forward-deployed-engineer-agentforce-data-cloud)), and getting there means passing through the developer tier that is shrinking while supply grows. Microsoft's routine low-code layer will commoditize. The pro-code layer above it, where low-code hits its limits (React code apps, TypeScript agents, MCP tool endpoints, Dataverse and Fabric integration, ALM and cost governance under consumption credits), is exactly where a JS engineer starts with an advantage. Those skills still carry over if the Microsoft bet underperforms. The clearer path is to specialize as a **pro-code Microsoft agent engineer**: Copilot Studio plus the M365 Agents SDK and Foundry, credentialed via AB-620 or AI-103 and then AB-100. Build TypeScript MCP servers as the neutral core skill that also lets you pivot to Agentforce later.

The DMV changes tactics, not the choice. The local Power Platform market is larger but leans on clearances, so your filtered feed will see a fraction of it. Seed `ATS_BOARDS` and queries toward commercial and civilian practices (Guidehouse, Deloitte, Booz Allen, Accenture Federal, Planet Technologies). Add "Copilot Studio" and "Power Platform developer" to `JOB_QUERIES`. Also decide deliberately whether "clearance eligible" postings should stay filtered, since sponsorship is how many DMV engineers get into the higher-paying cleared tier. Watch three signals that would shift this recommendation over the next 18 months. First, Gartner's predicted 2027 cancellation wave of agentic projects. Second, whether Copilot Studio reaches DoD IL5. Third, whether Salesforce's Agentforce pilots convert to production; credit replenishment, already about half of Agentforce bookings, suggests they are starting to ([Nasdaq](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/salesforce-q2-earnings-call-highlights)).
