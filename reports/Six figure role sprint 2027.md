# Ship AI features, then add a platform

**Target AI application engineering, applying through "full-stack + AI" titles, as your primary track. Keep ServiceNow developer as a gated backup.** Of the five tracks, the AI application/agent engineer scores highest (4.1 of 5 weighted). Pay is far above the $100K floor: Lightcast's median posted salary is **$153K for agentic-AI postings**. Demand is growing fastest, with **86,000+ US postings** mentioning agentic skills in 2025. It also has the highest federal billing ceilings: the **GSA "AI Engineer" median is $175/hr**, against $136/hr for "Software Developer". Its weakness is hireability. Mid-level postings are shrinking while senior ones grow, so the way in for a JS engineer is not the Python-leaning "AI Engineer" title. It is the "Full Stack Engineer – AI", "AI Product Engineer" and "Forward Deployed Engineer" postings that ask for TypeScript, React, Node, LLM SDKs and evals. That makes tracks 1 and 2 one search with one résumé. ServiceNow (3.6) is the best backup and the strongest bridge to a 2031 contracting firm: a GSA median of $164/hr, only 26 vendors, and buyers in VA, DC and federal civilian agencies. Its CSA+CAD certification path, plus the market's habit of placing cert-only switchers below $100K, makes it a worse first bet for an April 2027 deadline. Power Platform (3.0) and Salesforce (2.4) trail. In the DMV, most Power Platform work sits with federal contractors and often needs Secret. Salesforce's developer tier is oversupplied. The Public Trust decision is significant: about a third of the realistic DMV federal-adjacent pool opens up only if Public Trust postings are allowed. Your job agent currently hides some of those postings, and it also lets some Secret-required postings through (see the last section).

> **How to read the numbers.** "Measured" means a count or pay band read directly from a live board, ATS feed or API on 2026-11-09 or 2026-11-16. "Estimate" means analyst judgment or an extrapolation from a small sample. Scores in the comparison table are estimates anchored on measured inputs. LinkedIn guest-view counts ignored date, remote and level filters and are heavily duplicated, so they are treated as upper bounds only.

## AI application engineering pays most, but the door is the "full-stack + AI" title

Pay is not the binding constraint for the AI track; getting hired is. Every pay source is well above $100K. Lightcast puts all AI postings at a $141K median and IT postings generally at $125K ([Lightcast](https://lightcast.io/blog/tracking-the-agentic-ai-explosion-in-jobs)). A niche TypeScript agentic-jobs tracker shows **$162K–$252K mid-level** across 70 listings; the sample is small ([agentic-engineering-jobs.com](https://agentic-engineering-jobs.com/agentic-engineer-salaries/typescript)). Measured posted bands from 2026-11-16 ATS feeds confirm this at reachable employers. Capital One's 2–5-year "Software Engineer, Intelligent Foundations & Experiences" posts **$135.6K–$154.8K** ([listing](https://hw.glich.co/jobboard/jobs/software-engineer-full-stack-intelligent-foundations-and-experiences-at-capital-one-juu24a)). Temporal's remote SWE II posts **$118.4K–$194.3K** ([Ashby](https://jobs.ashbyhq.com/temporal/81cc8698-59f8-418a-85fd-1fc154b33ee4)).

Hireability is where the risk sits. Headline counts mislead. LinkedIn's "11,000+ AI Engineer TypeScript" US result had about 35 of its first 60 cards from one employer reposting the same remote role under each state capital ([LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/jobs/search?keywords=%22AI%20Engineer%22%20TypeScript&location=United%20States&f_TPR=r604800)). The real pool of distinct TS-centric AI roles is probably **hundreds to low thousands nationally (estimate)**. In the DMV, **20–22 of 60 visible generic "AI Engineer" cards (33–37%) were defense/IC primes or explicit clearance roles**, a measured page-one share ([LinkedIn DMV](https://www.linkedin.com/jobs/search?keywords=%22AI%20Engineer%22&location=Washington%20DC-Baltimore%20Area&f_TPR=r2592000)). Generic DMV "AI Engineer" roles at Capital One and the primes lean Python/ML. The structural market favors the JS angle, though. Lightcast finds **93% of AI adoption in postings comes from existing occupations adding AI skills**; only 7% comes from AI-titled jobs ([Lightcast](https://lightcast.io/blog/tracking-the-agentic-ai-explosion-in-jobs)). TS-first postings ask for the same things repeatedly: React/TypeScript plus Node, Vercel AI SDK or vendor SDKs for streaming and tool calling, MCP-style tool layers, and above all **eval harnesses** ([Vercel](https://vercel.com/careers/software-engineer-ai-sdk-5474915004); [StackBlitz via hotfix.jobs](https://hotfix.jobs/jobs/senior-applied-ai-engineer-at-stackblitz-faaece1a-5d37-4330-9845-c9c5ae6d7d94)). The DMV snapshot already contains titles built for this profile. Examples include Deloitte's "Software Engineer III – Full-stack Developer with Anthropic Experience" (McLean), Charles River Associates' "Full Stack Agentic Developer" (DC) and Capital One's "Full Stack Engineer 5 – AI Automation" ([LinkedIn DMV TS](https://www.linkedin.com/jobs/search?keywords=%22AI%20Engineer%22%20TypeScript&location=Washington%20DC-Baltimore%20Area&f_TPR=r2592000)).

The squeeze on mid-level is real and is the main threat to an April date. In software development, **senior roles are 69.3% of postings and entry-level 4.5%**. Since January 2025, mid-level postings across sectors fell **6.7%** while senior rose 13.5% ([Hiring Lab](https://hiringlab.indeed.com/2026/07/23/the-labor-market-is-tilting-toward-seniority/)). Engineering application-to-hire runs about **0.2%**, roughly 35 interviews per hire ([Gem](https://www.gem.com/resource/tech-hiring-guide)). That implies on the order of 500 cold applications per offer (an estimate, and the reason referrals and targeted outreach matter more than volume). Vendor recruiters report mid-level AI roles filling in **25–45 days** ([Axial Search](https://axialsearch.com/insights/ai-engineering-jobs)). Code for America warns of a "1–2 month, or longer" process ([CfA](https://job-boards.greenhouse.io/codeforamerica/jobs/8165204)). Working backward from April, the pipeline must be full by **early February 2027**. That leaves the next 8 weeks for building the portfolio, and applications should run alongside it, not after it.

For the 2031 business, the AI track ranks second on bridge value, not first, because federal AI is increasingly delivered through platform agents (Agentforce, Now Assist, Copilot Studio) rather than custom builds. The contracting research concludes that an AI engineer who also delivers on one platform would rank first overall. That is the reasoning behind the closing recommendation: get hired as an AI application engineer now, then add one platform in 2027–2028.

## Govtech full-stack is the easiest door, and it gives you Public Trust history

Track 2 is the closest skills match and the fastest to a $100K offer, but it carries the weakest bridge value. The govtech half has the most precise fits of any track. Oddball, a federal digital-services firm, posted on 2026-11-10 a remote **Front End Engineer at $100K–$130K** building React/TypeScript components for the VA Design System, and a **Senior Full Stack Engineer (DC area) at $130K–$175K**. Both require "U.S. citizenship and the ability to obtain and maintain a federal background investigation", which is Public Trust-type language, not Secret ([Oddball FE](https://job-boards.greenhouse.io/oddball/jobs/8200470003); [Oddball Sr FS](https://job-boards.greenhouse.io/oddball/jobs/8012571003)). Bellese's remote **Engineer II, Full Stack posts $108.7K–$125.3K** and requires the ability to hold a Position of Public Trust ([Lever](https://jobs.lever.co/bellese/06f71894-60f4-4119-9f48-b37d81f89ab3)). Excella posts Senior and Lead JavaScript Engineer roles in Arlington with no clearance text ([Excella](https://www.excella.com/careers/apply/?gh_jid=4748632005)). The postings name what sets a candidate apart in this market: **Section 508 / WCAG 2.2 AA**, USWDS, Storybook design systems and AI-assisted development.

The rest of the govtech market is thin and senior-heavy. Nava's only engineering openings are a 12-plus-year Ruby role ($135.9K–$153K) and a Java backend role ([Nava](https://job-boards.greenhouse.io/navapbc)). Skylight and Code for America list only senior-to-principal roles, and Ad Hoc's Ashby board showed **zero** postings (all measured 2026-11-16). The 2025 cuts explain much of this. GSA eliminated 18F in March 2025 ([Nextgov](https://www.nextgov.com/people/2025/03/gsa-eliminates-18f/403400/)), and trackers list more than **$16.2B in canceled contract value**, with "IT and digital services" a standout theme ([TBR](https://tbri.com/blog/doge-federal-it-vendor-impact-series-cgi-federal/)). The coalition firms are still winning work, consolidating rather than collapsing. Nava reportedly won a **$139M GSA Digital Services BPA** in September 2026 ([G2X](https://g2x.com/blog/daily-government-market-intelligence-09-11-2026)). Their boards suggest they are bidding new work more than adding delivery staff, so mid-level openings appear one at a time. Polling their feeds daily, which your agent can do, is the right tactic.

The commercial DMV half pays more and asks for no clearance. Posted bands on 2026-11-16 include Capital One (**$135.6K–$225.1K** across levels), Fannie Mae (**$96K–$161K**), Freddie Mac (**$127K–$191K**) and Aledade (**$134K–$182K**, remote) ([Capital One](https://www.capitalonecareers.com/job/mclean/full-stack-engineer-4/1732/100950228464); [Fannie Mae](https://www.themuse.com/jobs/fanniemae/sr-full-stack-engineer-aws); [Freddie Mac](https://builtin.com/job/senior-software-engineer/7346522); [Aledade](https://jobs.lever.co/aledade/c727d84a-427d-4bcf-bfa5-63f8743ac829)). The ID.me and Alarm.com feeds contained **0 of 48** and **0 of 91** postings mentioning clearance, respectively (measured). One title detail matters: a "web developer" title anchors near the **$99,520 BLS median**, while "software developer" sits at **$135,980** ([BLS](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/computer-and-information-technology/web-developers.htm)). Apply as a software or full-stack engineer.

As a bridge, full-stack alone ranks last. The GSA "Software Developer" category has **2,954 ceiling rates from 847 vendors**, and "Full Stack" has a $133/hr median ([GSA CALC](https://api.gsa.gov/acquisition/calc/v3/api/ceilingrates/?keyword=Full%20Stack)). A new firm selling only that would be one of hundreds of interchangeable bidders. A govtech job still builds something durable: Public Trust adjudication history, named agency past performance (VA, CMS, HHS) and references, all of which a 2031 GSA Startup Springboard application can count ([Winvale](https://info.winvale.com/blog/what-gsa-startup-springboard-program)).

## ServiceNow is the best bridge, but the wrong first bet for April

ServiceNow wins the long game. Its GSA ceiling median is **$164/hr against $136/hr** for generic developers, and only **26 vendors** hold ServiceNow titles, 25 of them small businesses ([GSA CALC](https://api.gsa.gov/acquisition/calc/v3/api/ceilingrates/?keyword=ServiceNow)). OneGov discounts of up to 70% on ITSM bundles run through September 2028, which pushes agencies to adopt more and creates implementation work ([Nextgov](https://nextgov.com/acquisition/2025/09/servicenow-gsa-strike-onegov-deal-drive-government-ai-adoption/407844)). Named civilian buyers include the VA (a **$527.6M** license task order), FDA, DOI and GSA OHRM ([GovConWire](https://www.govconwire.com/articles/metgreen-solutions-wins-5276m-va-task-order-for-servicenow-saas-license-entitlements)). In the DMV, VITA, VDOT and DC OCTO are all ServiceNow buyers ([ServiceNow newsroom](https://newsroom.servicenow.com/press-releases/details/2026/ServiceNow-accelerates-AI-powered-government-reinvention-across-nearly-all-50-U-S--states-with-new-customer-wins/default.aspx)). The platform also suits a JS engineer more each year. Development is Glide JavaScript, and the SDK's "Fluent" is a TypeScript-based DSL ([ServiceNow docs](https://www.servicenow.com/docs/r/zurich/application-development/building-applications/servicenow-fluent.html)). The May 2026 "Action Fabric" release opens the platform to any MCP-capable agent, with Anthropic as a launch partner ([ServiceNow](https://newsroom.servicenow.com/press-releases/details/2026/ServiceNow-opens-its-full-system-of-action-to-every-AI-Agent-in-the-enterprise/)).

The demand is DMV-heavy and partly open to non-cleared candidates. LinkedIn showed **727 DMV ServiceNow developer postings** against 4,000+ nationally ([LinkedIn DMV](https://www.linkedin.com/jobs/search?keywords=ServiceNow%20Developer&location=Washington%20DC-Baltimore%20Area)). Only about **15% of first-page DMV titles** explicitly required clearance (measured on n=20). With Public Trust allowed, an estimated **50–65%** are open to you, an inference rather than a measurement. Timing is what disqualifies ServiceNow as the primary. In 2026 exam vouchers no longer exist; a course must be completed to unlock each exam, which costs about **$300** ([ServiceNow Community](https://www.servicenow.com/community/training-and-certifications/2026-exam-vouchers-courses-exams-amp-retakes-major-changes/ba-p/3464930)). CSA plus CAD realistically lands around February 2027 (an estimate of 3–5 weeks plus 4–6 weeks part-time). KORE1 places "a fresh CSA plus solid JavaScript" in an **$80K–$105K junior band**; its mid band ($105K–$140K) assumes CSA, CAD and 3–5 years on the platform ([KORE1](https://www.kore1.com/servicenow-developer-salary-guide/)). No documented 2026 cert-only hire was found. The offshore floor is also visible. Infosys alone fields about **2,400 offshore or nearshore ServiceNow resources** ([Infosys](https://www.infosys.com/services/experience-transformation/documents/services-neat-assessment-2025.pdf)), and W2 contracts at **$40–$50/hr** appear on Dice ([Dice](https://www.dice.com/job-detail/100f9ceb-733a-4e89-ba73-4db975609497)).

Power Platform and Salesforce lose to ServiceNow as the backup for specific reasons. On Dice, **DMV "Power Platform" showed 297 postings, of which about 24 of the first 30 came from federal contractors**. Verified detail pages at SAIC, CACI and Andworx required Secret, and the estimated split is **35–50% Secret-or-higher** ([Dice](https://www.dice.com/jobs?q=Power%20Platform&location=Washington,%20DC,%20USA&latitude=38.9072&longitude=-77.0369&countryCode=US&locationPrecision=City&radius=50&radiusUnit=mi)). Copilot Studio is the exception: about 85–95% of cards show no clearance, but many are contract roles that require years of hands-on Copilot Studio work. AB-620, the developer-facing credential that replaced the retired PL-400, fits a JS engineer because it tests REST, MCP and A2A ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/credentials/certifications/ai-agent-builder-associate/)). It is a reasonable alternate backup if you prefer Microsoft. Salesforce shows **249 DMV developer postings, an estimated 55–65% needing no clearance**, but the developer tier is the most oversupplied segment. ICF's Public Trust roles ask for 3+ years of Salesforce plus Platform Developer I ([ClearanceJobs/ICF](https://www.clearancejobs.com/jobs/9032581/salesforce-developer-public-trust)).

## Primary, backup and the eight-week plan

**Primary: AI application engineer, entered through "Full Stack Engineer – AI", "AI Product Engineer", "Software Engineer (AI)" and "Forward Deployed Engineer" postings, plus govtech full-stack roles as a parallel lane on the same résumé.** It has the highest score, the most room on pay, and builds directly on what you already have: the job agent's multi-provider LLM chain, structured tailoring output and keyword-coverage scoring are an applied-LLM project. The pitch is "full-stack engineer who ships LLM features with evals". Employers then compare you to full-stack engineers with an AI edge, not to ML engineers.

**Backup: ServiceNow developer at DMV federal-civilian integrators (ICF, Booz Allen, Capgemini, MetroStar, LightFeather, Acuity, Dev Technology), Public Trust roles included.** It is gated on a decision date. If by **February 1, 2027** you have fewer than about three active interview loops (a heuristic, not a sourced threshold), take the CSA exam and pivot. The free on-demand course starts in week 7 below, so the pivot costs little. Whichever track lands the April job, **2027–2028 is the time to add a platform**, either ServiceNow (Now Assist/SDK) or Copilot Studio (AB-620). The combination of AI engineer plus platform is the strongest 2031 contracting profile in the research.

The plan runs about **Nov 17, 2026 to Jan 11, 2027**. Applications to the direct fits (Oddball FE, Bellese, Excella, Capital One SWE, Aledade, Temporal SWE II) start in week 1 and do not wait for the portfolio.

| Weeks | Build (portfolio evidence mapped to posting asks) | Job-search actions |

The 6–8-week window covers the applied-LLM gap (SDK tool calling, RAG, MCP, evals), which the research judges closable in that time. It cannot cover the ML or Python-depth gap behind generic "AI Engineer" roles, so skip those postings, and skip any posting whose description is Python-first.

## Five repo changes so the agent sees the right jobs

**1. The clearance filter drops Public Trust postings and lets some Secret postings through (both measured).** I ran `requiresClearance()` from `src/lib/filters.js` against posting text quoted in the research. It returns `true`, meaning drop, for Guidehouse's "Clearance Required: Ability to Obtain Public Trust", through the generic `clearance required` pattern, and for any "Public Trust clearance required". It returns `false`, meaning keep, for CACI's "Ability to obtain a minimum Secret security clearance" and Appian's "active Secret-level clearance", because `secret\s+clearance` does not match "Secret security clearance" or "Secret-level clearance". The fix is to split the patterns into *hard* ones (Secret and above, polygraph, DOE Q), which always drop, and *generic* ones, which drop only when the text does not mention Public Trust. I checked the replacement below against 16 posting-text cases, including both bugs, Oddball and Nava boilerplate, ICF, Bellese and "polymer/polyglot" noise. All 16 passed. The repo's own `npm test` suite has not been run against it.

```js
// src/lib/filters.js — Secret-and-above always disqualifies; Public Trust does not.
const HARD_CLEARANCE_PATTERNS = [
  /\b(?:top[\s-]*secret|ts\s*\/\s*sci)\b/i,
  /\bsecret\b(?:[\s-]+(?:level|security))*[\s-]+clearance\b/i,        // "Secret security/Secret-level clearance"
  /\bclearance\s*(?:level)?\s*[:\-–]?\s*(?:active\s+)?(?:secret|top\s+secret|ts\b)/i,
  /\b(?:secret|top.secret|ts\/sci)\s+cleared\b/i,
  /\bpolygraph\b/i,
  /\bpoly(?:graph)?\s+(?:required|clearance|test|exam)\b/i,
  /\b(?:ci|fs|full[\s-]scope|lifestyle|counter[\s-]?intelligence)\s*[-]?\s*poly(?:graph)?\b/i,
  /\b(?:w\/|with|and)\s*poly(?:graph)?\b/i,
  /\b(?:doe\s+)?q[\s-]clearance\b/i,
];
// Ambiguous phrases: only disqualifying when no Public Trust context is present.
const GENERIC_CLEARANCE_PATTERNS = [
  /\bsecurity\s+clearance\s+(?:is\s+)?required\b/i,
  /\brequires?\s+(?:an?\s+)?(?:active\s+)?(?:security\s+)?clearance\b/i,
  /\bmust\s+(?:have|hold|possess)\s+(?:an?\s+)?(?:active\s+)?(?:security\s+)?clearance\b/i,
  /\bclearance\s+required\b/i,
  /\bwith\s+(?:a\s+)?security\s+clearance\b/i,
  /\bactive\s+clearance\b/i,
  /\bclearance\s+eligib(?:le|ility)\b/i,
];
const PUBLIC_TRUST = /\bpublic[\s-]+trust\b/i;
const TITLE_ONLY_PATTERNS = [/\bcleared\b/i];

export function requiresClearance(job) {
  const title = job.title ?? "";
  const text = `${title} ${job.description ?? ""}`;
  if (TITLE_ONLY_PATTERNS.some((re) => re.test(title))) return true;
  if (HARD_CLEARANCE_PATTERNS.some((re) => re.test(text))) return true;
  if (GENERIC_CLEARANCE_PATTERNS.some((re) => re.test(text))) return !PUBLIC_TRUST.test(text);
  return false;
}
```

The new filter is deliberately conservative: "Public Trust **or** active Secret" still drops, because a hard pattern matches. Add the 16 cases to `test/filters.test.js`. If you want to switch Public Trust off again later, add an `ALLOW_PUBLIC_TRUST` flag in `config.js` and pass it in rather than reading `process.env` in the filter, as the project conventions require. The filter only works as well as the descriptions it sees. Dice cards showed no clearance text on any of 90 DMV cards, yet every federal-contractor detail page that loaded required at least Public Trust. The full descriptions from the ATS feeds are reliable input; aggregator snippets are not.

**2. `JOB_QUERIES`.** In `src/fetchers/ats.js`, `matchesQueries()` treats "developer", "engineer" and "software" as generic words. "full stack developer" therefore already matches "Full Stack Engineer", and synonyms add nothing on the ATS side. Each query does cost one call per run on JSearch, Adzuna and SerpApi, so keep the list short:
`JOB_QUERIES="full stack engineer,front end engineer,forward deployed engineer,ai engineer,product engineer,typescript engineer"`.
Add `servicenow developer` only when the backup is triggered on Feb 1. Leave out "web developer", which anchors pay below $100K, and "LLM engineer", which matches few postings and skews to ML.

## What the data cannot tell you yet

Several gaps limit confidence in the scores. **No reproducible Indeed, Glassdoor or Wellfound counts exist**, because those sites blocked automated fetches. LinkedIn guest view ignored date, remote and level filters, so **no clean US-remote count exists for any track**. The non-clearance and Public Trust shares (Power Platform 15–25% no clearance, Salesforce 55–65%, ServiceNow 50–65% open with Public Trust) are **extrapolations from 20–30-card samples**; they are not measured. **No source quantifies AI-coding displacement or offshore share for any of the five roles**, so the demand scores rest on growth signals and inference. Federal spending trends by platform are also missing: **no USAspending or FPDS time series was pulled**, so bridge rankings rest on GSA ceilings, which are not prices paid (task orders typically run 10–20% below ceilings ([GovConAPI](https://govconapi.com/gsa-labor-rates))), and on award anecdotes. Pay for many verified postings is missing because the Greenhouse feeds for Excella, Appian, ID.me, Alarm.com, Nava, Stripe, Anthropic and Dscout omit ranges. The **current status of OPM's US Tech Force** is unknown; it is a no-clearance federal fellowship paying $150K–$200K, and a 2027 cohort, if one opens, would fit your window ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2025/12/16/trump-tech-force-fellow-salary-range-experience-skills-requirement-companies-partners/)). **Two personal facts change the plan materially.** First, U.S. citizenship: Oddball's VA roles require it, and ICF and Ad Hoc ask for 3 of the last 5 years of U.S. residency, so "work-authorized" alone may close some Public Trust doors. Second, SBA eligibility (8(a), WOSB, SDVOSB, HUBZone) and DMV small-business certifications (SWaM, CBE) could reshape the 2031 path more than any role choice.

## Conclusion

The April deadline and the 2031 business pull in different directions, and that tension decides the order. The 2031 business favors a platform with pricing power and few competitors, which is ServiceNow. The deadline favors the skills you already have, which are TypeScript and LLM integration. Sequencing resolves it. AI application engineering gets you hired fastest at the highest pay. A platform added in 2027–2028 then turns that into the "AI engineer plus platform" profile the contracting research ranks first, in a federal market where license buying is centralizing and the money is moving into implementation and agents. Public Trust work at Oddball, Bellese, ICF or a similar firm also builds the agency past performance that GSA's Startup Springboard lets a founder count. Your job agent is both the first portfolio piece and the search tool. Fix its clearance filter first, because it currently hides Public Trust postings you can apply for and passes along Secret-required postings you cannot.
